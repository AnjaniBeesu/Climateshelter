Heat intelligence

Heat risk depends on temperature, humidity, sun, exertion, and access to water or shade. Use official local alerts for current conditions.

01 / Exposure

Hot surfaces, little shade

Dark pavement and dense built-up areas can stay hotter than nearby shaded spaces, especially after sunset.

02 / People

Higher-risk groups

Older adults, infants, outdoor workers, and people with certain health conditions may need more frequent cooling breaks.

03 / Response

Make a cooling plan

Check alerts, carry water, reduce strenuous activity in peak heat, and identify a nearby indoor cooling option.

Know the warning signs

Heat illness can become life-threatening. When in doubt, get medical help.

Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, or muscle cramps. Move to a cooler place, loosen clothing, cool the body, and sip water if fully alert. Seek medical help if symptoms worsen or do not improve.

Possible heat stroke · emergency

Confusion, fainting, seizures, or very high body temperature are emergencies. Call your local emergency number immediately. Move the person to a cooler place and actively cool them; do not give fluids if they are confused or unconscious.
